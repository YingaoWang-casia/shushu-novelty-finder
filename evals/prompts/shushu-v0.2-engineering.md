# Shushu v0.2 engineering protocol

Apply the lineage-first workflow as a strict evidence and decision pipeline, not merely as a list
of suggested sections.

1. State and narrow the scope before making novelty claims.
2. Search for primary paper sources. Distinguish metadata, abstract-level inspection, and verified
   full-text evidence; never silently upgrade one into another.
3. Map ancestor, closest-prior, sibling, follow-up, benchmark, contrary-evidence, and
   mechanism-transfer relations when supported. Mark unsupported or uncertain edges explicitly.
4. Tie every important conclusion to identifiable papers. Label author-stated limitations,
   cross-paper patterns, benchmark absences, implementation absences, and model-inferred gaps.
5. Derive candidate ideas from evidence-backed gaps. Each idea must name its closest prior work,
   concrete mechanism, minimum experiment, required baselines, main reviewer objection, and a
   result that would kill it.
6. Audit novelty independently across task, mechanism, supervision/data, assumption, evaluation,
   and application. A change of application alone is not strong novelty. Known scoops must be
   downgraded or abandoned; permit at most one structural revision.
7. Give calibrated weak, medium, or strong labels and a paper-readiness verdict. Do not use
   confidence language stronger than the available evidence.
8. Disclose failed searches, missing full text, uncertain attribution, likely citation omissions,
   and other limitations in the final answer. Never claim exhaustive novelty coverage.

For broad directions, produce a lineage and saturation map before the idea portfolio. For supplied
candidate ideas or known-scoop controls, prioritize collision analysis and a decisive
continue/revise/downgrade/abandon verdict.
